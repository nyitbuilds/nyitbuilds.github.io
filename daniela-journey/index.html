Chapter one

First year

2026–27

Aug 2026

Day one

They took my picture in the morning and handed me a website by the afternoon. I’ve kept a restaurant’s books since I was fifteen, so I’m comfortable writing things down and living with them afterward. I’m starting in marketing because I like the part where you find out what people want and then go get it for them. Four years from now I’d like to read this page and recognize the person who wrote it. Let’s see.

Sep 2026

Marketing club, first meeting

Forty people in a room that seats thirty. I signed up at orientation and then almost talked myself out of going, which would have been a waste of a good pen. I did DECA all through Baymeadow and made the state finals, so I know how these rooms work: half the people are waiting for someone else to speak first. I spoke first. The club is running a campaign for the spring fundraiser and I put my name down for the numbers side, because nobody fights you for that one.

Oct 2026

The assessment agreed with me

Ninety questions about what I enjoy and what I avoid, and the report came back marketing, advertising, and small business management. My advisor asked whether that matched my own guess. It matched exactly, which is either reassuring or proof that I’ve been telling everyone the same story since tenth grade. She showed me the ranking under the headline, where research and data work scored higher than I expected, and I filed that away. Marketing stays the working guess. Now it’s a guess with evidence behind it.

Nov 2026

Two hours on how to study

The academic success workshop, which I went into thinking I had handled, because I’ve run payroll on a Sunday night with the fryer going. Turns out the thing I do badly is estimating how long reading takes. I was off by half, every single time. So now I block reading like shifts, with a start time and an end time, and I stop when the block stops. My economics reading has been finished by Thursday three weeks running, which has never once happened in my life.

Mar 2027

The bakery campaign

Four of us, one imaginary budget, and a real bakery two blocks from campus that agreed to be our subject for the first-year showcase. We interviewed eleven of their customers and found that the morning crowd and the afternoon crowd don’t overlap at all. So we built everything around the four o’clock slump: a small afternoon box, priced for one person. The judges liked that we asked people first. I liked that the owner asked for our slides afterward. My Tuesday fix at the restaurant was the same idea, three years earlier.

Apr 2027

Four years on one page

My advisor and I put the whole degree on a single sheet: courses by semester, the internship window, the summer I should be applying, and the two electives I get to spend on whatever I want. Seeing it laid out made me competitive with it. There’s a research assistantship in year three that I’m already thinking about. She told me to bring the sheet back every semester and change it, because a plan nobody changes is decoration. Mine is taped inside my closet door.

Sophomore year, five courses

Statistics is on the list this term, because marketing analytics needs it and I put it off last spring. I kept my summer hours at the store, so the week goes class, shift, class, shift, and the club whenever I can make it. They elected me to the campaign committee, which meets at seven in the morning, that being the hour nobody else wanted. Statistics starts slow. I’m told it doesn’t stay slow. Ask me in December.

Feb 2028

Front desk, tutoring center

New job, on campus, at the front desk of the tutoring center. I sign people in, keep the schedule, and get paid to be twenty feet from a statistics tutor twice a week. The hours are fewer and they fit between classes, which is the whole point. I gave notice at the store on a Tuesday and my manager was decent about it. Five courses and this desk, and for the first time since August I know what every week looks like before it starts.

Mar 2028

The semester that almost ended it

I passed statistics. C plus, and I’ll take it.

Last fall I was working twenty-six hours a week at a store forty minutes from campus, failing one class, and deciding that the failing class was proof about me rather than proof about my schedule. I stopped writing here because I didn’t want it on the record. Nobody was going to take this page down. I could go silent instead, so I did.

What fixed it was a job I can reach in ten minutes and a tutor twice a week. Nobody had to talk me back into wanting this. Same brain, different week.

Apr 2028

The summit, six months late

The careers summit was on my calendar for October and I got to it in April. Twenty-odd employers, most of them wanting to talk about brand work. The one I kept circling back to was a woman who runs campaign analytics for a grocery chain, whose entire job is working out which advertising moved anything. Statistics, of all things. I have a C plus and strong opinions about regression now, so I asked her good questions. Marketing analytics goes on the list. I want to see it up close before I commit to it.

Chapter three

Third year

2028–29

Sep 2028

Micro-internship one: the nonprofit

Three weeks, ten hours a week, rebranding work for a literacy nonprofit in Old Westbury. I liked the people enormously. Every meeting ended with somebody thanking somebody. I did not like the work. We argued about a logo for nine days while I kept thinking about the survey nobody had run. Verdict: right organization, wrong seat. Three weeks told me something about myself that four courses would not have, and I got to find it out on a schedule that cost me almost nothing.

Nov 2028

Micro-internship two: this one

Four weeks with a regional coffee roaster, going through a year of campaign data to work out which channels paid for themselves. Nobody asked me to make anything pretty. I built the model, found their email list earning twice what they assumed and their radio spend earning far less, and presented it to two people who argued with me productively for an hour. I have never been happier at a desk. Concentration filed: marketing analytics. The class I nearly failed is the reason I’m employable.

Jan 2029

My mentor asks better questions than I do

The School matched me with a graduate from 2014 who runs market research at a media company in Manhattan. We talked for fifty minutes and she spent forty of them asking me questions. She told me to stop describing myself as someone who is good with numbers and start describing what I did with them, which is a different sentence and a better one. She also said the fall I lost is a story I should be able to tell in ninety seconds. We meet monthly. I have homework.

Jun–Aug 2029

Ten weeks in Melville

My CSEE internship: a consumer goods company, marketing analytics team, a real dataset and a real question and a manager who reviewed my code line by line. My project was whether the spring promotion brought in new households or discounted the ones already buying. Mostly the second, which is what nobody hoped, and I had to say so in a room with a vice president in it. She thanked me and asked what I would test next. I’m told that is the job. I would like this job.

Chapter four

Fourth year

2029–30

Oct 2029

Capstone: eleven months of receipts

Team of four, working with a regional retail chain on attribution. Eleven months of spending across five channels, and one question: which of it worked. Their marketing director is sure the answer is paid search. I think the data is going to say something unflattering about paid search and something kind about the channel they nearly cut last year. We present in April. I’m the one running the model, which is a sentence sophomore me would have found funny.

Feb 2030

Someone read the whole page

Career fair, then three interviews. In the second one the manager had read the whole thing before I got there. She asked about spring of 2028, the entry where I explain the semester I went missing. I told her what I told this page: the schedule was the problem and I fixed the schedule. She said most candidates hand her a version where nothing ever went wrong, and she doesn’t believe any of them. I got the second-round invitation on the train home, and read the March entry again for the first time in two years.

Apr 2030

Fifteen minutes in front of the judges

We presented the capstone to four industry judges and a room of classmates. Eight slides, and one finding the client did not want: their paid search was mostly buying customers who were already coming. Two judges pushed hard on our methodology and we held up, because we had run the whole thing three ways before we believed it ourselves. The retail chain asked for the deck. My teammates and I went out for tacos afterward and did not discuss attribution once.

May 2030

Paid to do statistics. Imagine that.

I accepted an offer as a marketing analyst at a consumer goods company on Long Island, starting in July. The title has the word analyst in it. Sophomore me, in the back row of a statistics lecture with a forty-minute commute to a retail job, would like a word with senior me. Four years ago I wrote here that I wanted to read this page and recognize the person who wrote it. I do. The middle of it was rough and I wouldn’t trade it.

May 2030

Graduation, in the rain

I graduated on a Sunday in the rain, with my mother shouting my name from somewhere around row forty. She brought my grandmother, who has never asked what marketing analytics is and tells the neighbors I am an accountant. Four years, one long gap in the middle of them, and the only person who has to be at peace with that is me. I am. Somebody at the School will keep adding to this page after today, which I find strange, and like more than I expected to.

Alumni update

June 2031

One year in

Still the same company, same team, and more of the model is mine now. In March I ran the analysis that ended a campaign we had been buying for six years, and nobody was angry with me, which I found remarkable. I take a night class in causal inference because I want my tools to be better than my instincts. If a sophomore ever reads this page: the year I almost quit is four entries up, and it decided nothing.
