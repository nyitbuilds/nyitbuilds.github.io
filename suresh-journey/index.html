01Aug 2026

Portrait day

They took my portrait at nine in the morning on my first day, before I had found the building where my classes were. The photographer told me to stop squaring my shoulders like a robotics team photo. By that afternoon this page existed with one entry on it, which felt premature for a person who had done nothing at the School of Management yet. The welcome session said the record fills up if you show up. I decided to test the claim over four years.

● Orientation week · Welcome to the School of Management

02Sep 2026

Three handshakes, four names

I went to the meet and greet planning to shake three hands and leave. I left with a peer mentor named Rohan, a junior in finance who told me which professors answer email on weekends and which do not. Two faculty members asked what I wanted to study and I said I did not know yet, which turned out to be an acceptable answer in September of freshman year. I wrote down four names that night. I have since taken classes from two of them.

● Meet and greet · Dean, faculty, peer mentors

03Oct 2026

The quiz agrees with me

I do not trust personality quizzes. I took the STRONG assessment and the interest profiler on a Tuesday afternoon with the attitude of a person humoring his advisor. Both came back pointing at analytics: investigative work, numbers with a business problem attached. My advisor watched me read the report and asked whether it surprised me. It did not. I was the kid who ran his robotics club budget in a spreadsheet nobody had asked for. Since the results agree with me, I am prepared to say the instrument works.

● STRONG interest assessment · Career interest profiler

04Nov 2026

The calendar method

The academic success workshop taught one method: every assignment gets a block on the calendar the day it is assigned, not the week it is due. I was skeptical for the full hour. Then I ran the method through midterms and finished a statistics project four days early for the first time in my life. I have used it every semester since. My roommate has watched me color code a Sunday afternoon and called it excessive. My grades from that term disagree with him.

● Academic success workshop · Time management

05Feb 2027

Reconnaissance at the career fair

My first career fair was reconnaissance. I wore the one jacket I own, walked the floor twice before speaking to anybody, and mostly listened. Three companies used the word analyst and meant three different jobs: one meant queries and dashboards, one meant forecasting, one meant a person who sits in meetings and builds the slides. I collected three business cards and wrote on the back of each what analyst meant at that table. That was the whole take, and it was more than I expected from an afternoon in a gym.

● Fall career fair · Center for Student Engagement and Enterprise

06Apr 2027

Four years on one page

My advisor and I built the four-year academic roadmap in one sitting: every required course, the two summers that matter, and the semester where statistics and the internship search collide. It is posted here now, so I cannot pretend later that I did not know. She said most students draft this in junior year and spend the next two semesters catching up. I would rather hold the map early and argue with it as I go. We changed one thing already and moved the second analytics course up a term.

● Academic roadmap · Filed with faculty advisor

07Sep 2027

Ninety seconds

The Pathways to Success Summit ran all day and I stayed for all of it. The session that mattered covered how employers read a student record, delivered by a recruiter who said she spends about ninety seconds on a candidate before deciding. Ninety seconds. I went back to my room and read this page as if I were her. I rewrote nothing. A dated list of things I did holds up better under ninety seconds than any paragraph about myself I could compose.

● SOM Pathways to Success Summit

08Oct 2027

Major confirmed

I declared Business Administration with a concentration in Business Analytics. The form took eleven minutes. The decision took fourteen months and is documented directly above it, entry by entry, which is a strange thing to be able to say. The roadmap updated itself the same week and the required courses rearranged into something tighter than what I had. My mother asked what business analytics is. I told her it is the part where you find out whether the thing you built made any money.

● Declaration of major · Certified entry, roadmap updated

09Nov 2027

I ran the oxygen math

Our team ran the HBR Everest simulation across two sessions and summited with all five climbers alive, which the facilitator told us is rare. I ran the oxygen math: variable consumption rates, weather delays that break the plan on round three, and one medic who could not make camp four no matter how we sequenced the ascent. Saying so out loud cost us a day and saved the team. Afterward the facilitator asked what we would take into a real project. Somebody has to deliver the arithmetic that ruins the plan.

● HBR Everest team simulation · Team of five

10Feb 2028

A job title I had not heard of

At the alumni mixer I met a 2014 graduate who does pricing analytics for a hotel group. I asked what she does on a Tuesday. She said she decides what a room costs tonight in eleven cities, and that most of the job is arguing with people who have a feeling about the number. We talked for forty minutes. I left with her email address and a job title I had never heard of in September and now wanted. She answers email, which I have tested twice since.

● Alumni and student mixer

11Mar 2028

First resume, in red ink

I brought a one page resume to the review campaign and left with it bleeding red ink. The reviewer cut my high school robotics team down to a single line and told me to put the club treasury on it instead, because two thousand one hundred dollars raised from local shops is a number an employer can check. She was right. Every bullet she kept had a quantity in it. Every bullet she cut described how I felt about the work. The second draft is on file with the career office.

● Resume review campaign · Career services

12Apr 2028

A Thursday in Hauppauge

I spent a Thursday shadowing an operations analyst at a logistics firm in Hauppauge. I expected a day of dashboards. I got four hours of a man on the phone with a warehouse in Edison and ninety minutes with the model that tells him which trucks to reroute. He showed me the spreadsheet behind the software, maintained for years by one analyst who left in 2019. The model is the easy part. Somebody has to know what the numbers describe when a driver calls in sick at six in the morning.

● Job shadow day · Long Island logistics firm

13Sep 2028

Vice president, analytics club

I was elected vice president of the analytics club and inherited a membership list of forty names and an attendance rate of nine. I fixed the meeting time first: Wednesday at six, when nobody has lab. Then I ran the term the way I would run a project, with four workshops booked before the first meeting so members could see the whole semester in advance. Attendance settled around twenty six. The president handles the room. I handle the calendar and the budget, which was my job in high school too.

● Analytics club · Elected vice president

14Oct 2028

Second place, and the better question

Four of us entered the corporate challenge with a retention model for a subscription business and we placed second. The judges were three industry analysts, and the one from the insurance company spent his entire question period on our assumptions rather than our results, which I now understand was the compliment. He asked why we dropped customers with fewer than sixty days of history. We had a reason for it, a decent one, though not the reason he would have used, and he took ninety seconds to explain his. I wrote it down.

● Corporate challenge · Judged by industry panel

15Jan 2029
Internship placement confirmed

Placed.

The Center for Student Engagement and Enterprise confirmed my summer placement in January: pricing analytics at a distribution company in Melville, twelve weeks, paid. The email arrived at 4:40 on a Wednesday and I read it three times. The placement came through the School, which means somebody in that office matched a junior who once ran oxygen math in a classroom simulation to a team that needed a summer analyst. Two and a half years of entries on this page did the introducing. I start on June 3.

● CSEE internship placement · Twelve weeks, paid

16Jun–Aug 2029

Twelve weeks in pricing

Twelve weeks in pricing analytics. The first three I spent untangling data, and I will describe them to nobody. In week four I found two product families priced off a cost table last updated in 2026, which the team knew about and had never had time to repair. I built the dashboard that flags it: current cost against list price, refreshed nightly, one screen. They still run it. My manager wrote my review around the phrase useful without supervision, and I intend to keep that phrase.

● Summer internship · Pricing analytics, one dashboard shipped

17Aug 2029

Everything a stranger can check

The advanced branding session ran two hours on LinkedIn and I went in expecting to be told to describe myself as passionate. Instead the facilitator had us link the record. Mine points here now, and the summer dashboard sits behind a link with the company data stripped out. My headline is nine words and names a concentration and a tool. The rule of the session was that anything on your profile should be something a stranger can check. Everything above this line can be.

● Advanced LinkedIn branding session · Portfolio linked

18Oct 2029

Capstone begins

My capstone team took the demand forecasting project with a regional retailer: nine stores on Long Island, four years of weekly sales. We met the client on a Tuesday and she told us her buyers order on instinct and are right more often than the software is. That is the problem in one sentence, since instinct does not scale past the buyers she already has. We have two semesters. The first deliverable is a baseline model that beats last year’s average, which sounds modest until you try it.

● Senior capstone · Regional retail client

19Feb 2030

Read before I spoke

Recruiting season ran four events in three weeks. I went to all four with the same one page resume and a link to this record. Two companies called back: a logistics group in Melville and the pricing team at an insurance carrier. Both asked about the summer dashboard, and one interviewer had already read the capstone entry above and opened with a question about my forecasting baseline. Being read before you speak is a strange feeling. It saved me the first ten minutes of introducing myself.

● Employer recruiting events · Two callbacks

20Mar 2030

Three certificates, in this order

I finished three competency certificates over the spring, each taught by alumni: data storytelling, financial modeling, and negotiation. I signed up for the negotiation session because it was the only one left on a Thursday, and it is the one I have used most since. The instructor, class of 2011, made us practice asking for a number and then waiting through the silence that follows. I was bad at the silence. I got better at the silence. The order I took them in is on the record too.

● Competency certificates · Alumni-led sessions

21Apr 2030

Slide six

We presented the forecast to four industry judges and the client. Our model beat the store average by eleven percent on the holdout weeks and lost to the buyers in two categories, and we put that on slide six rather than leaving it out. One judge said slide six was why he believed the rest of the deck. The client asked what it would take to run the model monthly. Three of us wrote the handoff document over the following two weeks, so somebody else can run it without us.

● Capstone presentation · Industry judging panel

22May 2030

Signed.

I signed with the logistics group in Melville: junior analyst, pricing and network planning, starting in July. The offer came on a Monday and I took four days to decide, which the recruiter said was fine and my mother said was theater. I used the four days to ask what the first eighteen months look like, and I got an answer specific enough to check later. Title on the record: junior analyst. I will report back in a year and we can see how the number moved.

● Offer accepted · Junior analyst, pricing and network planning

Commencement

I graduated on a Sunday in May with my family in row K and a cricket bat in the trunk of the car, for reasons that made sense at breakfast. Four years and twenty two entries before this one, none of them written by me alone. The keys passed that week from the managing editor to Alumni Relations, who keep an editor of their own and who will be the ones asking me every year what changed. Same standard, different office. I never held the keys and have no use for them, since a record I could edit myself would be a resume. The alumni chapter starts below.

One year in

One year in, my title changed to analyst II in June. Same team, pricing and network planning, more of the network than before.
