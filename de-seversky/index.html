1 Course design Class 1

Every student leaves able to trace a design choice to its effect on the P&L.

What it is
Learning objectives for each school, set out in Class 1. Each class ends with a 20-minute session. In the session, one team names a decision made that week and shows how the decision changed the budget or the forecast.
Why add it
In the syllabus, each team has a list of deliverables. No student has a list of things to learn. Last year, faculty ended each class with a similar session.
What students do
Teams take turns presenting the weekly decision. The other schools question them.
Students gain

An architecture student can explain what one more spa room does to the payback period. A finance student can explain why fire code on a historic stair can cap the number of guest rooms.

NYIT gains

NYIT can show accreditors (AACSB and NAAB) measured learning from an interdisciplinary course.

2 Marketing · Guest lecture Classes 2 and 3

Robert gets a revenue forecast based on what his own customers say they would buy.

What it is
A marketing professor gives a guest lecture in Class 2. Before Class 3, students interview recent customers of the mansion.
Why add it
In the syllabus, hotel market research is due in Class 3, but no team owns the task. Most published hotel data comes from hotels with hundreds of rooms. Robert's office would likely rent most room nights to wedding parties and event guests. Only those customers can say what they want and what they would pay.
What students do
Students sort the mansion's buyers into groups, such as couples, wedding planners and film location scouts. Each team interviews at least ten buyers. Each team then writes one value proposition per group.
Students gain

Students learn to test a business idea with buyers before anyone spends money on it.

NYIT gains

NYIT gets a revenue forecast built from interviews with its own customers.

Asks of Robert's office A list of recent clients willing to talk with students, and an introduction email from Robert's office.
3 Course design Class 2, tested again in Class 8

The Board can trust the verdict, because Robert and the trustee set the stop conditions before any numbers came in.

What it is
At Client Meeting #1, Robert and the trustee agree on three or four stop conditions. One example is the longest acceptable payback period. Another is the lowest acceptable room occupancy. Before Client Meeting #4, students test the forecast against each condition.
Why add it
Jai asked the team to determine financial feasibility “or not.” When students present one forecast, they tend to defend it. When the client sets the stop conditions first, students can report a “no” as a finding. The same meeting can also settle when the 36-month ROI period starts. The course description does not say.
What students do
Students put each main assumption in one labeled cell of the model. They move each assumption up and down. They record which moves cross a stop condition.
Students gain

Students learn to say which assumptions their conclusion depends on, and by how much.

NYIT gains

The Board learns which two or three assumptions matter most. The Board can then ask for more evidence on those assumptions before it commits money.

Asks of Robert's office Thirty minutes of Client Meeting #1.

4 Marketing · Follow-up Classes 3 to 6

Robert learns what couples would pay for a wedding with rooms upstairs, before NYIT commits to the restoration.

What it is
Students run a pricing survey. Each respondent chooses among wedding packages at different prices, some with overnight rooms and some without. Marketers call the method a choice-based conjoint study.
Why add it
NYIT is unlikely to recover the restoration cost from nightly rents on six or seven rooms. NYIT recovers the cost only if couples pay more for a package that includes rooms. No team in the plan measures that extra amount.
What students do
Students design the survey, send it through Robert's office, and estimate the price premium for each package. The premium goes into the revenue assumptions in Class 7.
Students gain

Students learn the method that firms use to price new products before launch.

NYIT gains

NYIT gets a measured price premium for the revenue line of the P&L, in place of a guess.

Asks of Robert's office Robert's office sends the survey link to past clients and recent inquiries. Students never see the contact list.

5 Operations · Guest lecture Class 4

Robert sees how many staff he would need for five-star service on a wedding night, and what they would cost.

What it is
An operations professor gives a guest lecture in Class 4. Class 4 is the week the class first discusses staffing and operating budgets.
Why add it
Forbes Travel Guide inspectors rate five-star hotels against up to 900 standards. About three-quarters of the score is for service. Guests expect a staffed desk at all hours, room service and quiet rooms. On a wedding night, the same kitchen and staff also serve the event downstairs. The plan includes a staffing budget. No team designs the service those staff would deliver.
What students do
Students map a guest's stay from arrival to checkout, step by step. They mark each point where a hotel guest and an event guest need the same staff, kitchen, stairs or parking. They then size the staff for a quiet night and for a wedding night. They size the spa the same way, from the number of treatments the rooms and therapists can deliver each day.
The marketing link
The lecture uses the service gap model from marketing (Parasuraman, Zeithaml and Berry). In the model, the gap that matters here is the distance between the service a hotel promises and the service its staff deliver on the busiest night.
Students gain

Students learn to design a service and to staff it.

NYIT gains

NYIT gets a staffing budget that follows from a service design. Labor is usually the largest operating cost of a luxury hotel.

Asks of Robert's office Current staffing levels for a typical wedding, and a 30-minute interview with the event manager.

6 Operations · Follow-up Classes 6 and 7

Robert sees, date by date, which current bookings the hotel and spa would conflict with.

What it is
Students build a calendar of the mansion's bookings from the last twelve months. Beside each date, they add the hotel's projected room nights and spa hours.
Why add it
The mansion already makes money from weddings, events and film shoots. A film crew may need the second floor. A wedding may run past midnight below guests who want to sleep. Under the project parameters, the team must keep revenue losses small. No class counts the conflicts.
What students do
Students tag each past booking with the rooms and staff it used. They mark every date where a hotel guest, a spa guest and an event need the same rooms or people. They price each conflict as lost revenue or as the cost to resolve it. They present the calendar at Client Meeting #3.
Students gain

Students learn to plan capacity from real booking records.

NYIT gains

NYIT learns what the conflicts would cost in a typical year, before it builds anything.

Asks of Robert's office Twelve months of booking records, with client names removed.

7 Course design Classes 7 and 8

NYIT counts only the money that the hotel and spa add to what the mansion earns today.

What it is
Students build two P&Ls. One shows the mansion as it runs today. The other shows the mansion with the hotel and spa. The value of the project is the difference between the two.
Why add it
In the syllabus, the P&L covers the hotel and spa alone. A stand-alone P&L gives the hotel credit for weddings the mansion would have hosted anyway. A stand-alone P&L also does not show the events lost during construction or the cost of moving the offices off the second floor.
What students do
Students start from the mansion's current results. They add the revenue from the interviews and the pricing survey. They subtract the conflict costs from the calendar and the events lost during construction.
Students gain

Students learn to value a project the standard corporate finance way, by comparing the business with the project and without it.

NYIT gains

NYIT sees how much more the mansion would earn than it earns today, after paying for the disruption.

Asks of Robert's office Summary revenue and cost figures for the mansion for the last three years.

8 Course design Class 15

Peter and the Board receive a two-page decision they can act on at their next meeting.

What it is
Students hand in a two-page memo with the final presentation. Page one contains the recommendation and its conditions. Page two contains the main assumptions and the evidence for each.
Why add it
Trustees often decide weeks after a presentation, at a board meeting, from papers in the board book. The students will not be there to explain their slides.
What students do
Students draft the memo in Classes 13 and 14. They revise it after Client Meeting #5.
Students gain

Students learn to write for a reader who will decide without them in the room.

NYIT gains

NYIT gets a document it can file with the Board's papers and reread when the Board takes up funding.

What the Board can decide

With the plan as written

Can NYIT restore six or seven second-floor suites and build a spa, and what would the work cost?

With the additions

Should NYIT build the hotel and spa?

What would customers pay, and how much would the project add to the mansion's current earnings?

Which results would stop the project?

How we chose these

We used seven rules to decide what to add and how to show it.

  1. The course produces two results: a decision for NYIT and a graduate who can make such decisions. Each addition improves at least one.
  2. The hotel and spa count only for what they add to the mansion's current earnings, after the cost of disruption.
  3. Every number in the final P&L needs a team that produces it from evidence.
  4. Each input arrives before the decision that uses it.
  5. The answer can be “no.” The client sets the stop conditions before the numbers come in.
  6. The additions fit the existing weeks and client meetings. Nothing in the plan as written is moved or removed.
  7. A reader can grasp the whole page in seconds and find any detail with one click.
